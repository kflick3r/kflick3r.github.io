Over the past two weeks, I worked on the fourth and final AI-powered study tool planned for StudySync, **Short Response Questions**!

After completing Multiple Choice, I wanted to build a study tool that would encourage students to explain concepts in their own words rather than selecting an answer from a list of options.

I didn't make much progress during Week 9, but I was able to return to development during Week 10 and get the initial version of Short Response working.

The interface now allows students to generate questions from their course notes, write responses, and compare their answers against sample answers and key points generated by Gemini.

<!--more-->

# **What I Worked On**

I started by defining what I wanted the Short Response tool to accomplish.

Like the other StudySync features, Short Response uses student-provided course notes to generate study materials. For this tool, Gemini generates up to five questions, each with a sample answer and a list of key points that a strong response should address.

I wanted the questions to focus on explaining, comparing, and applying concepts rather than simply recalling definitions.

Some of my main requirements were:
- Generate questions that can reasonably be answered in 2–4 sentences.
- Focus on important concepts from the original notes.
- Include questions that encourage conceptual understanding.
- Provide sample answers that students can use to check their understanding.
- Identify 2–5 key points for each question.
- Avoid repetitive questions or information unsupported by the notes.
- Generate fewer than five questions when the notes don't support five meaningful questions.

I also added validation checks to make sure Gemini returns the expected JSON structure, including nonempty questions, sample answers, and key points.

# **Building the Short Response Interface**

Most of my development time went toward building the Streamlit interface.

I wanted Short Response to feel consistent with Multiple Choice, so I reused the same general approach to question navigation and progress tracking.

Students can now move through one question at a time, type their responses into a text box, and click Check Answer when they're ready.

The interface includes:
- A question counter showing the current question and total number of questions.
- A progress bar tracking how many questions have been checked.
- A text box for writing responses.
- A Check Answer button that requires a nonempty response.
- Previous and Next buttons for navigating between questions.
- Session state management to preserve responses when navigating between questions.
- Answer locking after a response has been checked.

I also made sure that generating a new set of questions resets the previous answers and progress.

One of my goals was to make the interface straightforward enough that students could focus on explaining the material rather than figuring out how to use the application.

# **Adding Self-Assessment**

For the initial version, I decided to use a simple self-assessment approach rather than immediately implementing AI-generated feedback.

After submitting a response, students can view Gemini's sample answer and a list of key points that their answer should include.

Students can then select checkboxes to indicate which key points they believe they covered in their response.

The application displays a summary showing how many of the key points they selected.

This provides a way for students to reflect on their understanding without requiring another Gemini API call.

However, there is an important limitation: **the application currently relies on students to evaluate their own responses.**

For example, a student could submit an incomplete or incorrect answer and still select every checkbox. The application would report that they identified all the key points, even though their actual response might not demonstrate that understanding.

I think self-assessment can still be valuable, but I don't want students to confuse their own assessment with an objective evaluation of their answers.

# **Challenges and Reflection**

The biggest challenge with Short Response is figuring out how to evaluate open-ended answers fairly.

With Multiple Choice, the application can compare the student's selected answer against a known correct answer. Short Response is more complicated because students can explain the same concept in many different ways.

A response might demonstrate a good understanding of the material without using the exact terminology or wording found in the sample answer.

I don't want StudySync to penalize students for giving valid alternative explanations. At the same time, I want it to recognize when an answer is incomplete, contains misconceptions, or doesn't actually address the question.

This is why I decided to separate building the interface from implementing AI-powered evaluation.

Getting the basic functionality working first allowed me to focus on the user experience without trying to solve every challenge at once.

I've also been thinking about the difference between helping students assess their own understanding and having an AI model assess it for them. Both approaches have potential benefits, and I may eventually explore how they could work together.

For now, I'm happy with the progress I've made on the initial version. **All four planned StudySync study tools now have an initial implementation**, even though Short Response still needs additional functionality and testing.

# **Weeks 9–10 Progress**

Over the past two weeks, I:

- Developed the initial Gemini prompt for Short Response question generation.
- Added structured JSON output and validation checks.
- Implemented generation of up to five questions with sample answers and key points.
- Built the Short Response interface in Streamlit.
- Added text input, question navigation, and progress tracking.
- Implemented session state management to preserve student responses.
- Added answer locking after submission.
- Implemented sample answer and key-point displays.
- Added self-assessment checkboxes.
- Identified limitations of self-assessment and planned the next stage of development.

# **Next Steps**

Next week, I plan to focus on **implementing Gemini-powered evaluation of student responses**.

Instead of relying entirely on students to identify which key points they covered, I want Gemini to evaluate their written answers and provide more detailed feedback.

My goal is for the evaluation to include:
- A score from 0–5 based on accuracy and completeness.
- What the student explained correctly.
- Important concepts that were missing or could be improved.
- An example of a stronger answer.
- An overall assessment of the student's understanding.

I also want to make sure Gemini recognizes valid explanations that use different wording from the sample answer.

Once the evaluation is working, I'll focus on completing the results page and testing the quality and consistency of the feedback.

I'm looking forward to seeing how this feature develops. Short Response is probably the most challenging of the four study tools, but I think it also has the potential to be one of the most useful for helping students identify gaps in their understanding.
